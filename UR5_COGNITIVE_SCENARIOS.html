Is it cognitive? — video scenarios with a control and a measured verdict

Stage vocabulary of this build: the human phases of annotations/ur5_human_phases.json (VL-JEPA aligned to a human annotation of this recording, in-sample). "Cognitive" is used here in a testable sense: the twin holds expectations (what comes next, when, what it looks like), acts on them before the fact, notices when reality breaks them, explains why, recovers, and knows when it does not know. A reactive system — a change detector on the very same latents — has none of this, and it runs as the control in every scenario. Every UR5 scenario is a manipulated version of the real recording pushed through the full stack (pixels → VL-JEPA → committed stage → Stage-JEPA plan → validation gate): inputs the twin has never seen. Thresholds are not tuned per scenario: an alarm means "more surprising than anything in the execution the twin studied, by 25 %" (next_stage_metrics.json › thresholds), and the unmodified recording must produce zero alarms. Two scenarios that do not work after one execution are kept as limits, with the reason, next to the work-cell scenario where the same machinery works after eight.
Scores: 9 of 9 scenarios pass their measured criterion; the two limits are expected failures. Caveats are stated per scenario. Videos: 15 fps dashboards rendered from the twin's actual read-outs (cognitive_scenarios.py).

5 · Knows what it does not know PASS

Scenario: a different scene (the simulated work cell, 10 s) fed to the UR5 twin

7 · Violated expectation: a stage cut out (work cell, unseen execution) PASS

the twins name the expected and the observed stage
Scenario: 1.3 s cut out of the unseen execution (robot A's TRANSFER): every agent jumps to a later stage
Claim: the twins (trained on 8 other executions) recognise the transition the cut creates as one their process never contains (p ≈ 0.00 for the arriving stage, named in the explanation); at least one stream raises the order alarm within 1 s; no alarms anywhere else on this unseen execution
Control: a change detector on the same per-agent vectors: the step across the cut is of the same size as ordinary stage changes
Measured: robot_A: no alarm, robot_B: no alarm, human: alarm (0.10 s); alarms on the unmodified unseen execution: {'robot_A': 0, 'robot_B': 0, 'human': 0}; robot_A: change detector max inside / outside 1.76 / 1.01; robot_B: change detector max inside / outside 1.80 / 1.16; human: change detector max inside / outside 1.60 / 0.62
twin: “robot_A: after TRANSFER expected PLACE (p=0.99); PLACE arrived (p=0.99) | robot_B: after LIFT expected TRANSFER (p=0.99); RETURN arrived (p=0.00) | human: after WALK_IN expected PICK_FROM_SHELF (p=1.00); WALK_OUT arrived (p=0.00)”

Caveat: perception gates cognition: the human's twin (perception 94 % on unseen executions) alarms at once; the robot twins (72–80 %) see the same never-seen transitions (p = 0.00) but their own perceptual flicker on held-out executions produces surprises of the same size, so the calibrated threshold cannot separate the cut from noise
